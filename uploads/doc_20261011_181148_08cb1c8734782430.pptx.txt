Introduction: The Modern Scrolling Problem

Kartik Malav  |  KID: 26967
2
• Infinite Scroll
Apps keep showing new content instead of giving us a clear stopping point. This makes scrolling feel endless.
• It Is Not Just Weak Willpower
Many apps are designed to keep our attention. So getting stuck in scrolling is not simply a personal failure.
• Mindless vs. Intentional Use
A quick 2-minute check can easily become a long scrolling session when we are not using the app with a clear purpose.
• Why Soft Skills Matter
Self-awareness, attention control, and emotional regulation can help us manage our digital habits better.

--- SLIDE 3 ---
The Psychology Behind the Scroll

Kartik Malav  |  KID: 26967
3
• Dopamine Loops
Dopamine is linked with anticipation. Each swipe can create the feeling that something interesting might appear next.
• Variable Rewards
Content appears unpredictably. Sometimes we find something exciting, so the brain keeps checking for the next rewarding update.
• No Clear Stopping Cue
Older media had natural stopping points, like the end of a chapter or page. Endless feeds remove many of these breaks.
• The result: stopping becomes a conscious choice instead of a natural ending.

--- SLIDE 4 ---
What Endless Scrolling Can Cost Us

Kartik Malav  |  KID: 26967
4
• Attention Fragmentation
Frequent switching between short pieces of content can make it harder to stay focused on longer tasks.
• Lower Patience
Constant quick content may make slow, difficult tasks and learning feel less comfortable.
• Decision Fatigue
Consuming many small posts can use mental energy and leave less clarity for important decisions.
• Less Real-Life Connection
Passive screen time can replace some meaningful conversations and active social interaction.

--- SLIDE 5 ---
Reclaiming Focus: Simple Steps

Kartik Malav  |  KID: 26967
5
• Technology should support our goals, not control how we spend our time.
• 1. Self-Awareness
Pause and notice how you feel before opening a social media app. Ask yourself what you actually want to do.
• 2. Create Friction
Set app time limits, turn off unnecessary notifications, and make mindless scrolling less automatic.
• 3. Take Active Breaks
Replace some screen time with real breaks, movement, hobbies, study time, or conversations.
